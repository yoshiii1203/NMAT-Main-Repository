# CHED MEMORANDUM ORDER (CMO)

**No. \_\_\_, s. 2026**

**SUBJECT:** AMENDMENT TO SECTION 17.5 (NMAT CUT-OFF SCORE) OF CHED MEMORANDUM ORDER (CMO) NO. 18, SERIES OF 2016, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS "POLICIES, STANDARDS AND GUIDELINES FOR THE DOCTOR OF MEDICINE (MD) PROGRAM"

---

In accordance with the pertinent provisions of Republic Act (RA) No. 7722, otherwise known as the "Higher Education Act of 1994" and by virtue of Commission En Banc Resolution No. 230-2026 dated April 20, 2026 entitled, "A Resolution Amending the Admission Policies for the Doctor of Medicine Program under CHED Memorandum Order No. 18 (s.2016), Providing for Updated National Medical Admission Test Requirements for State Universities and Colleges and Private Higher Education Institutions", the Commission hereby issues the following policy:

## I. RATIONALE

Pursuant to the mandate of the Commission on Higher Education (CHED) to promote relevant and quality higher education and ensure alignment of medical education with national health needs, there is a need to revisit the National Medical Admission Test (NMAT) cut-off score policies under Section 17.5 of CHED Memorandum Order (CMO) No. 18 (s. 2016).

This amendment seeks to:

- Strengthen quality assurance mechanisms in medical education;
- Promote institutional accountability through performance-based standards;
- Enhance social equity and access to medical education for underserved sectors; and
- Safeguard the global competitiveness of Philippine medical education.

## II. LEGAL BASES

This issuance is anchored on:

- R.A. No. 7722, otherwise known as the "Higher Education Act of 1994";
- CMO No. 18 (s2016); and
- Other relevant laws, rules, and regulations.

## III. SCOPE AND COVERAGE

This CMO applies to all Higher Education Institutions (HEIs), including State Universities and Colleges (SUCs) and Private HEIs (PHEIs), offering the Doctor of Medicine (MD) program.

## IV. AMENDMENT TO SECTION 17.5 (NMAT CUT-OFF SCORE)

Section 17.5 of CMO No. 18 (s. 2016) is hereby amended to read as follows:

### A. For State Universities and Colleges (SUCs)

1. **For Filipino Students:** The mandatory NMAT cut-off score is maintained at the 40th percentile for Filipino applicants to SUCs, while affirming the authority of SUC Governing Boards to set higher NMAT thresholds consistent with institutional capacity, competitiveness, and quality assurance objectives.

   SUCs may admit applicants with NMAT scores between the 30th and 39th percentile, provided that the applicant belongs to underrepresented and disadvantaged groups (e.g., a resident of a Geographically Isolated and Disadvantaged Area [GIDA] or belongs to an Indigenous Peoples [IP] community). These applicants must present sufficient official documentation establishing such personal circumstances, such as a certification from the National Commission on Indigenous Peoples (NCIP) in the case of IP applicants, and a certification from either the Department of Health or the concerned local government unit in the case of GIDA-resident applicants.

2. **For Foreign Students:**

   a. Enrollment is capped at ten (10) slots per incoming freshmen class, effective Academic Year 2026-2027; and

   b. The adoption of a composite/weighted ranking system for foreign applicants (e.g., 60/40 or 70/30 NMAT-to-other criteria such as GWA, interview, and/or other validated measures) to ensure a holistic assessment and the protection of state-subsidized slots for Filipino citizens.

### B. For Private Higher Education Institutions (PHEIs)

1. **Guidelines for the NMAT Cut-Off.** The NMAT, as a mandatory admission requirement for all applicants, shall be retained, while allowing PHEIs to peg their own NMAT cut-off; Provided, That the NMAT cut-off should be at least 30th percentile, and that:

   a. The chosen NMAT cut-off shall be applied equally to local and foreign applicants;

   b. Only PHEIs with a Physician Licensure Examination (PLE) performance at a rate above the average national passing percentage for the last five (5) years can avail of this adjusted cut-off of 30th percentile for both local and foreign students;

   c. PHEIs performing below the average national passing percentage for the last five (5) years shall have a minimum cut-off of 40th percentile;

   d. PHEIs are strongly reminded and encouraged to implement rigorous, authentic formative and summative assessments and clear progression standards to ensure readiness across year levels and to safeguard program quality; and

   e. Admission of foreign nationals shall not be construed as a guarantee of timely completion of the degree.

2. **On Quality and Accountability.** This CMO reiterates that institutional autonomy must align with outcomes-based accountability, including compliance with CHED monitoring and evaluation mechanisms and continuous improvement anchored on student performance and licensure outcomes. A PHEI's privilege to set its NMAT down to the 30th percentile is directly tied to its PLE performance. If a PHEI's PLE passing rate falls below the average national passing percentage for three (3) consecutive years, CHED shall revoke this autonomy and reinstate the 40th percentile baseline for that specific institution.

## VI. MONITORING AND EVALUATION

CHED shall regularly monitor compliance with this policy through:

- Annual evaluation of NMAT cut-off implementation;
- Review of PLE performance data; and
- Institutional reporting and audit mechanisms.

Non-compliance shall be subject to appropriate sanctions in accordance with existing CHED policies.

## VI. TRANSITORY PROVISION

HEIs shall be given a period of one (1) academic year from the effectivity of this CMO to fully comply with the revised NMAT cut-off requirements.

## VII. REPEALING CLAUSE

All provisions of CMO No. 18 (s. 2016) and other issuances inconsistent with this CMO are hereby amended or repealed accordingly.

## VIII. EFFECTIVITY

This CMO shall take effect after fifteen (15) days following its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation.
